## FIX Items (must fix — improvement needed)
1. **Telegram E2E inaccuracy (line 41)** — "Messages are end-to-end encrypted. The platform doesn't know what's in them, so it can't selectively remove content." → This is factually wrong. Telegram only uses E2E in "Secret Chats"; regular chats are stored on Telegram's servers. The argument still works if nuanced. **Suggested rewrite**: "Telegram's Secret Chats use end-to-end encryption, but even its regular architecture gives the platform limited visibility into message content. Either way, the government has demanded compliance with due diligence requirements that Telegram says it can't meet."

2. **No specific case or court named (throughout)** — The entire article discusses a legal fight between India and Meta but never names a court, filing, date, or government proceeding. Line 117 references "court filings" but the article never cited one. **Fix**: Add at least one anchor point in the opening or first substantive section. Example: "The dispute traces to [specific proceeding/filing/government document] — though as of [date], no court has ruled." Even a vague anchor is better than nothing.

3. **Global Comparison section is still thin (lines 76-86)** — The previous review flagged this and the draft still only names laws by title. Line 80 quotes Section 230's text, which is good, but line 82 says the DSA "takes a different approach" without explaining *what* that approach actually is. **Suggested fix**: Add one specific mechanism per jurisdiction. For the DSA: "The DSA created a 'Very Large Online Platform' category that triggers additional obligations — including transparency reports on algorithmic recommendations and a right for users to opt out of algorithmic curation — but crucially does not treat algorithmic curation as publishing." For the UK: mention the Online Safety Act's risk-assessment requirement if space allows.

4. **Closing sections overlap (lines 101-117)** — "Where This Goes" and "The Bottom Line" make nearly the same point. Line 103: "the question isn't theoretical anymore" and line 115: "the answer to that question will determine what kind of internet India gets." **Suggested fix**: Merge these into one stronger closing section. The line "That's not a policy debate. It's a product decision." (line 117) is the best line in the closing — build the section around that, then cut the repetition.

5. **₹2-5 crore figure is unsourced (line 57)** — "according to estimates from legal firms specializing in digital regulation" is non-specific. Either name the firm or remove the sourcing claim and use a hedge: "independent estimates suggest compliance costs could reach ₹2-5 crore annually for a mid-sized platform."

6. **"Let's do the numbers" is a cliché pivot (line 51)** — The transition "Let's do the numbers" is a well-worn journalistic pivot that reads formulaic. **Suggested rewrite**: "Here's the financial reality:" or "The math doesn't work at scale."
